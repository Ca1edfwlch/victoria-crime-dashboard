# Victoria Crime Dashboard (2016–2025)

An interactive dashboard analyzing **10 years of recorded crime trends in Victoria, Australia**, built using R, ggplot2, Plotly, and flexdashboard.

## Project Overview

This project explores how crime rates in Victoria changed between 2016 and 2025, focusing on overall trends, offence categories, and patterns observed during and after the COVID-19 pandemic.

The dashboard consists of two sections:
- **The Big Picture:** Overall crime trends, key statistics, and comparisons across offence divisions.
- **The Story:** A closer look at family violence, theft, and residential burglary trends.

## Key Findings

- **Overall crime:** The recorded crime rate in 2025 approached its 2016 level, despite substantial changes across offence categories.
- **Family violence:** Family violence-related offences increased, including breaches of family violence orders.
- **Theft:** Motor vehicle and retail theft declined around the pandemic period before rebounding.
- **Residential burglary:** Residential non-aggravated burglary declined by approximately 45% between 2016 and 2025.

## Tools & Technologies

- **R:** Data cleaning, transformation, and analysis
- **dplyr & tidyr:** Data manipulation and aggregation
- **ggplot2 & Plotly:** Interactive data visualization
- **flexdashboard:** Dashboard development

## Data Source

**Crime Statistics Agency Victoria (CSA)** - Recorded Offences by Offence Type, Year Ending December 2025.

## Limitations

Recorded crime statistics may be affected by reporting practices. Observed changes during COVID-19 do not necessarily indicate causation. Population estimates derived from published rates should be validated.
